About Me

My interest in economics grew out of a longstanding engagement with communities navigating migration, economic opportunity, and development.

I have worked with a grassroots NGO in El Salvador since I was fourteen years old. I became interested in how economic development is experienced by individuals and communities from the bottom up.

This motivated my study of economics and international development at Tulane University as an undergrad student. I continued to work with the NGO throughout college.

At twenty-one, I traveled by myself to Ethiopia to better understand the immigrant communities I encountered in Washington, DC.

I have been fortunate to work at think tanks such as the Center for Global Development and the Washington Office on Latin America in Washington, D.C. I met people who are hardworking, funny, and curious about helping the world become a better place.

These experiences have continued to shape my approach to economics. My work asks questions about economic development from the grassroots up, while also considering how migration and economic change affect people in the United States. I am particularly interested in understanding these questions not only from the perspective of migrants, but also in terms of the experiences and economic interests of people in places such as Texas, Ohio, Tennessee, Nevada, and North Carolina, to name a few.

Ultimately, I am interested in research that connects individual decisions and lived experiences to broader economic questions about labor markets, migration, development, competitiveness, and public policy.
