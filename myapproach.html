My Approach

My interest in economic development began with a commitment to understanding how economic opportunities are shaped at the grassroots level. Over time, experiences in El Salvador, Ethiopia, Washington, D.C., and across the United States have shaped how I think about migration, labor markets, and economic development.

Grassroots start

I began working with a grassroots NGO in El Salvador at age 14. I have continued volunteering with and leading U.S. delegations for the NGO for the past 15 years. This has deeply shaped my understanding of economic development as a communal undertaking.

Beyond the Americas

At 21, I traveled to Ethiopia by myself to better understand the experiences of immigrant communities I encountered in Washington, D.C. The experience broadened my perspective on migration.

Policy and practice

I have been fortunate to work at think tanks such as the Center for Global Development and the Washington Office on Latin America in Washington, D.C. These experiences gave me the opportunity to work alongside people who are hardworking, funny, and curious about helping the world become a better place.

Scholarship in action

I've supported EdD students developing their dissertations in El Salvador, facilitated workshops for an RCT done in collaboration with the International Rescue Committee in Kampala, and conducted qualitative interviews with migrant service providers in Lima. It's only so often you get to ask someone who is a Congolese refugee what he would have done if he had been the U.S. President during 9/11.. ah, where side conversations take us.

Economic development at home

My interest in economic development also extends to communities in the United States. I am interested in understanding economic opportunity from a grassroots perspective while considering the experiences of people working in construction in southern California, the restaurant industry in North Carolina, and the hospitality industry in Kansas.

Economics and research

These experiences have shaped my interests as an economist. My research focuses on migration, labor markets, and economic development, with an emphasis on how institutions and economic conditions shape people's choices and opportunities.
